## 6. Business formation & registers (high-level, cited)

*Not tax/accounting advice. Structure choice is **OPEN / verify** with IR / accountant / adviser; visa filter may further constrain (→ §9).*

### Structures (formation posture)

- **EVIDENCE (sole trader):** Business.govt.nz (MBIE) states a sole trader does **not** need to register the business with a government agency to start; the person must have an IRD number and **tell Inland Revenue** they are working for themselves; they must hold any **licences / qualifications** the trade requires. NZBN is **optional but recommended**. ([Business.govt.nz — Becoming a sole trader](https://www.business.govt.nz/business-stage-or-type/sole-traders/becoming-a-sole-trader). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **EVIDENCE (company):** To incorporate a NZ company, Companies Office requires an online services account, **name reservation**, then incorporation application. Published fees on the help page: **$10 + GST** to reserve a name; **$118.74 + GST** to apply to incorporate; **20 working days** after name reservation to complete (incl. director/shareholder consents). Annual return fee published as **$49.74 + GST**. On approval, Certificate of Incorporation is issued and company details become **public** on the Companies Register. ([Companies Register — Incorporating a company](https://companies-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz/help-centre/starting-a-company/incorporating-a-company/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **EVIDENCE (GST threshold):** Business.govt.nz: must register for GST as soon as you think you’ll earn more than **$60,000 in 12 months** (sole trader, contractor, partnership, or company); voluntary registration possible below that. GST charged at **15%** when registered. ([Business.govt.nz — GST](https://www.business.govt.nz/tax-and-money/guide-to-business-tax/gst). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **HYPOTHESIS:** For early digital wedges, sole-trader + IR notification is the lightest formation path *procedurally* — but **visa conditions** (esp. employing others / occupational registration) and **sector licences** can matter more than Companies Office friction. Do not treat “easy sole trader” as clearance to trade.

### Public registers (transparency layer)

- **EVIDENCE:** Companies Office administers multiple registers contributing to a transparent business environment, including the **Companies Register**, **Limited Partnerships**, **Financial Service Providers Register (FSPR)**, **Personal Property Securities Register**, **Charitable Trusts**, **Incorporated Societies**, and others. ([Companies Office — All registers](https://www.companiesoffice.govt.nz/all-registers/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **EVIDENCE:** Other business/entity types (incl. **sole traders**, partnerships, trusts, limited partnerships) can be searched via Companies Office business-register tools; sole traders/partnerships/trusts may choose to keep some information private — search results then show only what they made public. ([Limited Partnerships help — Searching our other registers](https://lp-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz/help-centre/searching-the-registers/searching-our-other-registers/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **EVIDENCE (FSPR):** The FSPR is a searchable register of financial service providers; managed by Companies Office / MBIE; holds details on financial advice providers/advisers, banks/NBDTs, credit providers, FX, fund managers, insurers, issuers, etc.; some professionals (e.g. lawyers, chartered accountants, tax agents, real estate agents) have **exemptions** when financial services are a necessary part of practice. ([Companies Office — Role of the FSPR](https://fsp-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz/about/the-role-of-the-fspr/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **INTENT / implication:** Public registers support **trust and discoverability** for later wedges (honest trader identity, NZBN, company search) — aligned with anti-spam / Latent-class originality, not doorway anonymity.

## 7. Consumer / fair trading / competition posture (cited)

### Fair Trading Act (FTA) — claims, ads, online trade

- **EVIDENCE:** Consumer Protection: FTA prohibits misleading/deceptive practices; covers advertising, pricing, promotions, sales techniques; illegal to mislead on prices, fake discounts, hidden costs, **false or unsubstantiated claims**; also covers unfair contract terms, uninvited direct sales, product safety. Applies to **anyone in trade**, incl. overseas suppliers into NZ and **online sales**. Online sellers who operate as traders must make clear they are traders (incl. via intermediary sites e.g. Trade Me). Commerce Commission enforces. ([Consumer Protection — Fair Trading Act](https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/general-help/consumer-laws/fair-trading-act). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **EVIDENCE:** Unsubstantiated claims: traders need **reasonable grounds** for a claim at the time it is made — breach even if the claim later turns out true (puffery exception for obvious exaggeration). ([Same FTA page](https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/general-help/consumer-laws/fair-trading-act). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **EVIDENCE:** Commerce Commission: FTA concerns trader behaviours likely to mislead on price/performance/quality; covers unfair contract terms, door-to-door, pyramid schemes, product safety; infringement notices possible for (among other things) traders who **fail to disclose they are a trader when selling online**; max penalties for most offences cited as **$200,000** per offence (individual) / **$600,000** (business). ([Commerce Commission — Laws we enforce](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/about-us/our-role/laws-we-enforce/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **EVIDENCE:** Commerce Commission consumer overview: FTA applies to advertising in **all forms** (online, print, TV, social media) and all dealings with consumers; businesses must describe goods/services accurately, including overall impression and material omissions. ([Commerce Commission — Your rights as a consumer](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/consumers/your-rights-as-a-consumer/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)

### Consumer Guarantees Act (CGA) — goods & services quality

- **EVIDENCE:** CGA sets minimum guarantees for products/services sold by businesses in NZ for personal/household use; remedies include repair/replace/refund; applies including online; services guarantees include **reasonable care and skill**, fit for purpose, reasonable time, reasonable price (if not agreed). Attempting to contract out of CGA can be an FTA offence. ([Consumer Protection — Consumer Guarantees Act](https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/general-help/consumer-laws/consumer-guarantees-act). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **INTENT / implication for models:** Any **paid digital product**, subscription, agency service, or connect fulfillment sold to consumers inherits CGA-style quality expectations — design wedges for **real utility**, not disposable doorways.

### Commerce Act — competition posture

- **EVIDENCE:** Commerce Act purpose: promote competition for the long-term benefit of consumers. Prohibits restrictive trade practices incl. **cartels / price fixing**, taking advantage of market power, arrangements that **substantially lessen competition**; also merger control and Part 4 regulation of certain monopoly-like sectors (electricity lines, gas pipelines, some airports); market studies power. ([Commerce Commission — Laws we enforce](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/about-us/our-role/laws-we-enforce/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **HYPOTHESIS:** For a solo early wedge, Commerce Act risk is usually low vs FTA/CGA — but **connect/hybrid** models that coordinate pricing among suppliers, or grow toward market power in a thin NZ niche, should treat competition rules as a later-stage screen (Step 4/5), not ignore them.

### Privacy (audience / signup lever)

- **EVIDENCE:** Privacy Commissioner: agencies operating in NZ that collect personal information must follow the Privacy Act — includes **companies, small businesses, and sole traders**. Personal information = any info about an identifiable person (emails, notes, photos, etc.). Every NZ business/organisation must appoint a **privacy officer** (can be the operator). ([Privacy Commissioner — Complying with the Privacy Act](https://www.privacy.org.nz/responsibilities/your-obligations/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **EVIDENCE (business.govt.nz summary):** Collect only what is needed; tell people how/when/why; keep safe; don’t keep longer than needed; don’t send overseas without checking protection; privacy statement required when collecting. ([Business.govt.nz — Protecting customer and employee information](https://www.business.govt.nz/operations/it-risks-and-scams/protecting-customer-and-employee-information). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **INTENT:** Owned-audience levers (newsletter, signup, CRM) are **first-class** under this program — and institutionally **privacy-shaped**, not optional hygiene.

### Trust context (from Steps 0–1, still binding)

- **EVIDENCE (recap):** NZ Consumer Survey 2024 — high online purchase rates coexist with security concern and limited habitual trust of sales claims (see Step 0/1 cites). Combined with FTA/CGA, **misleading SEO/ads copy is structurally misaligned** with both law and measured trust.

## 8. Sector licensing clusters relevant to creative models (qualitative map; not counsel)

*Qualitative map only. Presence of a cluster ≠ “operator needs this licence.” Applicability = **OPEN / verify** with the named regulator / licensed adviser. Not exhaustive.*

| Cluster | What it tends to gate | Typical creative-model touch | Thickness (qualitative) |
|---------|----------------------|------------------------------|-------------------------|
| **A. Universal trade rules** | Anyone “in trade”: FTA claims, CGA if consumer supply, Privacy if PI collected, GST if turnover threshold | **All** families (product / media / connect / hybrid / format) | Baseline — always on |
| **B. Company / FSP transparency** | Public company details; FSPR if providing certain **financial services** | Product/media that touch investing, credit, insurance, FX, “financial advice”; connect-to-advisers | Medium–high if financial; low if pure non-financial content |
| **FAP / financial advice** | Regulated financial advice to **retail** clients → FMA Financial Advice Provider licence (or under another FAP) + FSPR registration pathway | Productised “what should I invest / insure / borrow” advice; lead-gen into advice that crosses into regulated advice | **High** — treat as wedge-killer unless intentionally licensed |
| **C. Trades / building** | Restricted building work → **Licensed Building Practitioner (LBP)**; electrical → EWRB practising licence; plumbing/gas/drainlaying → PGDB registration + practising licence | Connect/marketplace for home trades; content that **arranges or performs** restricted work | **High** for doing/supervising work; lower for pure info **if** claims stay accurate (FTA) |
| **D. Real estate agency work** | Real estate agency work generally requires REA licence (salesperson / branch manager / agent) + prescribed qualification; fit-and-proper tests; REA notes visa holders must check Immigration NZ themselves | Property lead-gen / “we’ll sell your house” agency activity; not mere general property content | **High** for agency work; content-only may still hit FTA |
| **E. Health / therapeutic claims** | Medicines Act / Medsafe advertising controls; therapeutic purpose claims pull products into therapeutic rules; ASA Therapeutic and Health Advertising Code (industry self-reg + Medsafe collaboration) | Media/product wellness claims (“cures”, “treats”, “prevents”); influencer health ads | **High** for therapeutic purpose claims; general lifestyle content lower but FTA still applies |
| **F. Employment / hiring** | Employer registration with IR if hiring; employment standards (out of deep scope here); **visa** may separately bar employing others (→ §9) | Connect/hybrid that scales via employees vs contractors | Medium legally + **visa-critical** for this operator track |
| **G. Screen / entertainment production** | Separate industry regs exist (e.g. Screen Industry Organisations register listed by Companies Office) — **not** mapped as a thesis path | TV/format production franchises | **Out of north-star scope** (Latent = metaphor only) |

### Cluster notes (cited anchors)

- **Financial advice (B):** FMA pages state anyone giving **regulated financial advice to retail clients** must hold or operate under a **Financial Advice Provider (FAP)** licence; FSPR registration for the relevant service precedes FMA licence application. ([FMA — Financial Advice Provider licensing](https://www.fma.govt.nz/business/services/financial-advice-provider/financial-advice-provider-licensing/); [FMA — FAP overview](https://www.fma.govt.nz/business/services/financial-advice-provider/). Accessed 2026-09-11 via public pages / search; re-check when unfreezing.)
- **Building / trades (C):** MBIE describes LBP scheme: from Mar 2012, practitioners must be licensed to carry out or supervise **restricted building work** (design/construction critical to integrity — foundations, framing, roofing, cladding, etc.). PGDB / EWRB described as competency regimes for plumbing/gas/drainlaying and electrical. ([MBIE — Regulating building professionals and practitioners](https://www.mbie.govt.nz/building-and-energy/building/regulating-building-professionals-and-practitioners). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **Real estate (D):** REA: licence required to undertake real estate agency work; age 18+, fit and proper, prescribed qualifications (e.g. NZ Certificate in Real Estate (Salesperson) Level 4 for salesperson). **“REA doesn’t give advice about visa restrictions… contact Immigration NZ.”** ([REA — Required criteria and qualifications](https://www.rea.govt.nz/real-estate-professionals/apply-for-your-licence/required-criteria-and-qualifications-to-work-in-real-estate/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **Therapeutic advertising (E):** Medsafe: devices/products claimed to have therapeutic properties must comply with Medicines Act/Regulations; advertising also subject to FTA/Commerce Act; TAPS pre-vetting available via ANZA. ([Medsafe — Advertising of Medical Devices](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/Regulatory/DevicesNew/12Advertising.asp); [Medsafe GRTPNZ Part 7 PDF](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/regulatory/Guideline/GRTPNZ/Part7_Advertising_of_therapeutic_products.pdf). Accessed 2026-09-11.) ASA Therapeutic and Health Advertising Code is **Tier B** industry code supporting the same cluster. ([ASA — Therapeutic and Health Advertising Code](https://asa.co.nz/codes/codes/therapeutic-and-health-advertising-code-new/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)

### Model-family constraint sketch (INTENT framing)

| Model family | Usually lighter if… | Usually heavier if… |
|--------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| **Product** (digital tools, downloads, courses) | Non-financial, non-therapeutic utility; accurate claims; CGA-ready fulfillment | Investment/credit “advice”; health cure claims; consumer guarantees ignored |
| **Media** (audience, content, ads) | Useful local/sector info; substantiated claims; privacy-aware lists | Unsubstantiated SEO promises; therapeutic ads; undisclosed trader status |
| **Connect** (lead-gen, manual broker, marketplace) | Manual intros where *licensed parties* perform regulated work; honest matching claims | Operator personally performing restricted trades/RE/financial advice; price-fixing among suppliers; **hiring staff** under constrained visa |
| **Hybrid / format** | Distinctive mechanic + distribution that stays outside thick licences | “Entertainment franchise / TV production” path (out of north star); regulated advice wrapped as content |

## 9. Immigration / work-rights filter (verify-yourself; operator PSW = OPEN)

### Hard writing rule (program lock)

- **INTENT:** Never invent what this operator may do from the words “post-study work” alone.
- **Operator status (INTENT / operator-stated, not Immigration NZ EVIDENCE about this person):** Indian citizen; NZ **post-study work** visa holder (charter §5).
- **All wedge implications:** encode as **OPEN / verify** via [Immigration New Zealand](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/) and/or a **licensed immigration adviser** / counsel. Personal visa conditions, qualification pathway (degree vs non-degree), and endorsement text control.

### High-level Immigration NZ context (category pages — not personal advice)

- **EVIDENCE (category description):** Immigration NZ states that a Post-Study Work Visa may allow stay/work for **up to 3 years**, depending on qualification. For a **degree level 7 or above** completed over at least **30 weeks** full-time study, the published page says the holder may do **any work, in any job, anywhere in NZ**, provided: work is legal; not commercial sexual services / related business; and any **occupational registration** required for that work is held. ([Immigration NZ — Staying to work after finishing your study](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/study/after-you-finish-your-study/staying-to-work-after-finishing-your-study/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **EVIDENCE (sole trader / business ownership — category page):** Same page states holders **can be a sole trader or own a business**, but if operating a business as an owner, they **cannot employ other people** — directly (hire yourself) or **indirectly** (e.g. business manager hires). ([Same Immigration NZ page](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/study/after-you-finish-your-study/staying-to-work-after-finishing-your-study/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **EVIDENCE (non-degree pathway):** Below degree level, eligibility may require a qualification on the eligible list and a job **related to studies**, with conditions specified on the visa. ([Same page](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/study/after-you-finish-your-study/staying-to-work-after-finishing-your-study/); [Immigration NZ — Qualifications needed for a Post Study Work Visa](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/study/after-you-finish-your-study/qualifications-needed-for-a-post-study-work-visa/). Accessed 2026-09-11.)
- **EVIDENCE (policy manual colour):** Immigration NZ Operations Manual WD3.5 distinguishes open work (Bachelor’s / L8–10 pathway language) vs occupation-restricted work for certain non-degree pathways. ([INZ Ops Manual WD3.5](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/opsmanual/85192.htm). Accessed 2026-09-11. Policy dates change — re-check.)
- **UNKNOWN / OPEN for this operator:** Exact conditions on **this** visa label; whether sole-trader / company / contracting / employing is permitted **for them**; interaction with occupational licences. **Do not answer from this card.**

## 10. Implications for invent-or-adapt models (not niche pick)

1. **Baseline always on (EVIDENCE → INTENT):** FTA (accurate, substantiated claims; trader disclosure online) + CGA (if selling to consumers) + Privacy (if collecting emails/PI) apply across **all** model families. Latent-class originality (mechanic / audience / distribution) must still be **honest trade**.
2. **Formation is cheap; permission is not (HYPOTHESIS):** Companies Office / sole-trader setup is procedurally light relative to **visa**, **FAP**, **REA**, **trades**, and **therapeutic** clusters. Do not confuse register friction with regulatory clearance.
3. **Licence thickness should steer survey order, not lock a niche (INTENT):** When scoring invent-or-adapt candidates in Steps 4–5, down-rank wedges that require the operator to **personally** hold thick licences **or** hire staff — until OPEN items clear. Up-rank distinctive mechanics that sell **non-regulated utility**, **audience**, or **route demand to already-licensed parties**.
4. **Connect ≠ marketplace mandate (INTENT):** Connect remains one family among many. Institutional map shows connect often collides with **occupational licensing + employment/visa**. That is a reason to survey **product/media/hybrid** with equal seriousness — not a reason to clone Urban Company.
5. **Financial & health claims are asymmetry traps (HYPOTHESIS):** Thin content that drifts into “you should invest/borrow X” or “this cures Y” can jump clusters from media → FAP/Medsafe overnight. Keep claim discipline in research and later copy.
6. **Privacy is part of the owned-audience moat (INTENT):** Signup/newsletter levers require privacy-officer posture and transparent collection — compatible with build-in-public trust; incompatible with harvested spam lists (also method-banned).
7. **Competition law is a late-stage screen for most solo wedges (HYPOTHESIS):** Prioritise FTA/CGA/Privacy/visa first; revisit Commerce Act if coordinating supplier prices or dominating a thin national niche.
8. **Latent bound (INTENT):** Distinctive creative value ≠ TV production franchise. Screen/entertainment production regs are noted only to keep that path **out** of the north star.
9. **Next cards:** Competitors/substitutes across model families (Step 3) should note who already holds trust **and** licences; ecosystem archetypes (Step 4) should include an **institutions row**; wedges (Step 5) must carry the **PSW OPEN/verify** filter.
