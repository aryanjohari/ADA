## 1. Question

What fixed YAML, what weekly steps, and what decision rules let one agent turn provable facts into a few public pages, then read Search Console, then decide, for one portfolio or one shop?

## 2. Short answer

**POLICY.** An SME, in the OECD statistical band, is a firm employing up to 249 persons. Inside that band the narrow slice this agent serves is one origin, one audience sentence, one result, and the facts the operator can prove. A solo portfolio and a one-shop barber sit in the micro band on the staff test. The portfolio result is a reply from someone who might hire. The barber result is a booking or a call from someone the shop can actually serve.

**POLICY.** One file shape. The fields are site, audience, aim, places, offer, proof, contact, the action counter, and a queue. Each queue item is one fact, one reader question as the title, one source, and one URL once published. The queue holds at most 12 items. That 12 is this design. Google publishes no such count.

**POLICY.** The file may create one page per fact that is not already on a URL. The weekly pass wakes, reads the file, publishes at most one new page, then reads Search Console for every URL already live, after the delays Google states. The only decisions are patch this same URL, or leave it. A query, a click count, or an impression count moving up or down does not open a URL.

**POLICY.** The action counter is the pass. The four Search Console numbers are a report. This card states no conversion percent.

## 3. What an SME is, and what the slice is

### The wide band

**EVIDENCE.** OECD, [Enterprises by business size](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/indicators/enterprises-by-business-size.html), accessed 2026-10-01. An enterprise is a legal entity with the right to conduct business on its own. The most common size criterion is the number of people employed. SMEs employ fewer than 250 people. Micro enterprises employ fewer than 10. Small enterprises employ 10 to 49. Medium-sized enterprises employ 50 to 249. Large enterprises employ 250 or more. The same page says this indicator is measured as the number of employees in the manufacturing sector. That sentence is the indicator’s unit. It is not a rule that a portfolio or a barber must be a manufacturer to be read against the size bands.

**EVIDENCE.** OECD, “Defining SMEs for statistical purposes,” in [C/MIN(2017)8](https://one.oecd.org/document/C/MIN(2017)8/en/pdf), Meeting of the Council at Ministerial Level, 7–8 June 2017. A standard international definition of SME does not exist. For statistical purposes the OECD refers to SMEs as the firms employing up to 249 persons: micro (1 to 9), small (10 to 49), and medium (50–249). Some countries use different conventions. The same note says data for Switzerland, Japan, and the United States in that document do not include non-employers.

**EVIDENCE.** Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC, [EUR-Lex 32003H0361](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reco/2003/361/oj), Article 1 and Annex Article 2, OJ L 124, 20.5.2003, accessed 2026-10-01. An enterprise is any entity engaged in an economic activity, whatever its legal form. That includes self-employed persons and family businesses engaged in a craft. The SME category is enterprises which employ fewer than 250 persons and which have an annual turnover not exceeding EUR 50 million, and/or an annual balance sheet total not exceeding EUR 43 million. A small enterprise employs fewer than 50 persons and its annual turnover and/or balance sheet total does not exceed EUR 10 million. A microenterprise employs fewer than 10 persons and its annual turnover and/or balance sheet total does not exceed EUR 2 million. Annex Article 5: the headcount is annual work units. Staff includes employees, persons deemed employees under national law, owner-managers, and partners engaging in a regular activity and benefiting financially. The recommendation’s own Article 2 says the Annex ceilings are maximum values. Member States, the EIB, and the EIF may fix lower ceilings. They may apply only the employee criterion, except in fields governed by State-aid rules.

The two “fewer than 250” sentences are the same staff ceiling written two ways. The OECD statistical line also says “up to 249.” The EU line adds the money ceilings. This card does not collapse those into one legal test.

### Where these two sites sit

**POLICY.** A solo portfolio is one owner-manager. On the EU staff list that person is staff. One full-time person is inside the micro staff ceiling, fewer than 10, and inside the SME staff ceiling, fewer than 250. On the OECD breakdown, 1 person is micro (1 to 9), which sits inside “up to 249.”

**POLICY.** A one-shop barber, in this slice, is one shop whose staff including the owner-manager is under 10. That is the same micro staff band, inside the SME staff ceiling of fewer than 250. This card did not count a real shop’s employees. A shop that employs 10 or more has left this micro reading. It can still be an SME until the staff ceiling of fewer than 250, or until it misses the EU money ceilings. It is outside the slice in §3 below once it is no longer one origin with a short list of provable facts.

**UNKNOWN.** Turnover and balance-sheet total for either site. The EU micro test also requires annual turnover and/or balance sheet not exceeding EUR 2 million. This card measured neither figure. It does not invent one.

**HUNCH.** A one-person portfolio and a one-chair shop are the sort of enterprise the EU text already names: a self-employed person, or a family business in a craft. That reading places them in micro on the staff test. It does not certify either firm for an EU programme. Certification would need the money figures and, where the firm is partnered or linked, the other firms’ figures. Those figures are **UNKNOWN**.

### The narrow slice

The SME band is the wide set: up to 249 employed, and on the EU test the money ceilings above. A firm of 249 people can be an SME and still be outside this agent.

**POLICY.** The slice is four things, all present before a wake runs:

1. One origin. One site. The portfolio origin is `github.com/aryanjohari/aryan-portfolio`. A shop is a different origin with the same file shape. Card 07. Card 10’s unattended checkout write and push apply to the portfolio repo only.
2. One audience sentence. Who the page is for, written by the operator.
3. One desired result, stored as `aim` and counted as `actions`. The portfolio result is a reply from someone who might hire. The barber result is a booking, or a call, from someone the shop can actually serve.
4. A short list of facts the operator can prove. That list is the queue. The agent does not extend it.

`aim` is one of `hire`, `prove-shipping`, `educate`, `book`, `call`. Card 10 already allows `hire`, `prove-shipping`, and `educate` on the portfolio. `book` and `call` are the shop’s result. Local service-area intent is not an aim on the portfolio. Card 10.

## 5. Which pages the file may create

**EVIDENCE.** Google, [Spam policies for Google web search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), doorway abuse and scaled content abuse, as card 02 locked them (page last updated 2026-08-28 UTC on that card). Doorway abuse includes city or region pages that funnel visitors to one page, generated pages whose job is to funnel, and substantially similar pages that stand in for a hierarchy. Scaled content abuse is many pages generated primarily to manipulate rankings, with little or no value for a user, however they are produced. One stated example is generative AI used to make many pages without added value. Google states no count for “many.”

**EVIDENCE.** Google, [Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), as card 10 locked it. Making a separate page for every query variation, primarily to manipulate rankings or generative AI responses, violates the scaled-content policy. A high quantity of pages does not make a site more relevant.

**EVIDENCE.** Google Business Profile Help, [Tips to improve your local ranking on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en), accessed 2026-10-01. Local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity. The three factor headings on that page are Relevance, Distance, and Prominence. Relevance is how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for. Distance is how far each business is from the customer who is searching. If the customer does not share a location, Google uses what it knows about their location. Prominence is how well-known a business is, including how many websites link to it and how many reviews it has. More reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking. The page states no review count that means the business has passed. It says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. This card does not operate a Business Profile. The same three factors are why the shop page may state only a place, an offer, and hours the operator can prove.

**POLICY.** One page per fact that is not already on a URL. The title of that page is the item’s question.

**POLICY.** The barber gets one page for the shop. That page carries the suburb actually served, what is different about the shop, the hours, and how to book or call. Another place gets a page only when a fact changes: a different door, different hours, or a different offer, each one provable. A second suburb with the same door and the same hours stays on the first URL. A set of suburb pages that send the reader to that one shop page is the doorway pattern card 02 already refuses.

**POLICY.** The portfolio gets one page per shipped thing, or per real question a reader would ask about that work. A contact link is on the page only when `aim` is `hire`. When `aim` is `prove-shipping` or `educate`, the page has no contact link. Card 10: if the portfolio chain config has no mail-and-call pair, the blog page still has no call to action. This file does not invent the missing half of that pair. Local service-area intent stays off this origin. `places` on the portfolio file is empty.

**POLICY.** The weekly pass publishes at most one new page. The 12-item ceiling is the file’s size. The one-page week is the pass’s speed. Card 06: the weekly list is this design’s schedule. It is not a cadence Google states.

**FEASIBLE.** The portfolio origin can serve a public HTML URL. Card 04 measured HTTP 200 HTML at `https://aryan-portfolio-one-kappa.vercel.app` on 2026-09-29. Whether any queue URL is indexed is **UNKNOWN**. Card 05.

## 6. The weekly campaign

**EVIDENCE.** Google Search Console Help, [About Search Console data](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/96568), accessed 2026-10-01. Collected data is published in intervals. Normally, collected data should be available in 2–3 days. It can take up to a week to generate data for a newly created site, or a site newly added to Search Console. If the Performance report shows no data, one reason that page gives is that people are not yet clicking the site in search results. Card 05: Google states no day count from the publish of one URL until the first impression, click, or query. That count stays **UNKNOWN**.

**EVIDENCE.** Google, [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide), last updated 2025-12-10 UTC, accessed 2026-10-01. Some changes might take effect in a few hours. Others could take several months. In general, wait a few weeks to assess whether the work had beneficial effects in Search. Not every change has a noticeable impact. Those sentences are about assessing Search. They are not this weekly pass, and they are not a day until the first impression. The same guide says Google Search does not use the keywords meta tag, and that there is no magical word-count target.

**POLICY.** One pass per week. The week is this design’s clock. It is not the starter guide’s “few weeks,” and it is not the Search Console week. Card 10’s 24-hour sleep between chain stages stays. This pass does not run every stage in one breath, and it does not emit many pages.

| 2. Read the YAML | Site, audience, aim, places, offer, proof, contact, actions, and the queue, up to 12 items. | Treat a query, a click count, or an impression count as one of those fields. |
| 3. Publish at most one new page | If one fact has an empty `url`, that fact may become the one new page this week. The title is the question. The body comes from the source on that row. On the portfolio, card 10’s chain may write and push that one page when the critic has passed. On any other origin, the first public URL still waits until a person says yes. Card 07. Card 10. | A second new URL in the same week. A page for a fact that already has a URL. A page whose source is missing. A city or suburb grid. |
| 4. Read Search Console for every URL already live | After the delays above. For each live URL, when a property contains it, read the card-05 Pages row: clicks, impressions, CTR, and average position. Empty stays empty, with the card-05 reason that applies. A new property can show nothing for up to a week. Collected numbers are normally visible in 2–3 days. There is still no stated day from this URL’s publish until the first impression. | A results scrape. An unfiltered property chart read as this URL’s number. A dash read as position zero. Connecting a property this card did not connect. |
| 5. Decide | Patch this same URL, or leave it. Card 06’s order: a second URL for the same question is a duplicate, so leave the live URL; leave when there is no new fact that would substantially change the page; patch when gather has that new fact and the gate passes. The modified date changes only on that patch. | Open a new URL. Let a Search Console number flip the mark. Invent the fact the patch would need. |

**POLICY.** The rule that forbids a new URL. A rise in a query’s impressions, a fall in clicks, a higher CTR, a lower average position, or the reverse of any of those, leaves the URL set as it is. The agent patches the same URL only when a new provable fact would substantially change that page. It otherwise leaves the page. Card 05: those four numbers cannot pass or fail the page, cannot choose the next subject, and cannot prove a patch caused the change. Card 06: a query does not choose a subject. Card 07: the top query is not the question, and the `main` planner’s “create or split dedicated landing page for this query cluster” is refused. Card 10: a Search Console query is not a title.

## 7. What the four Search Console numbers count

**EVIDENCE.** Google Search Console Help, [What are impressions, position, and clicks?](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7042828), accessed 2026-10-01. The four metrics on the performance reports are impressions, clicks, average position, and click-through rate, calculated as clicks ÷ impressions. Position is shown only for Google Search results. Click, impression, and position data are attributed to the canonical URL Google selects, though in some cases the data might be assigned to the actual URL. A redirect after the user lands does not change which URL received the impression or the click. Search Console does not include data from experiments in Search Labs.

| Field | What it counts | What it does not count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clicks | A click that sends the user to a page outside Google Search, Discover, or News. A click on a link to an external page in an AI Overview counts. The same is true in AI Mode. An AMP click that opens the AMP reader counts. Clicking a different link counts for each link. | A click that stays on Google. A query-refinement click, which runs a new Google query. A second click on the same link after the user returns. Search Labs experiments. A booking, a call, or a reply. |
| Impressions | A link the user saw, or could have seen, in Search, Discover, or News. In the general case, an item on the current page of results counts whether or not it was scrolled into view, when the user does not have to click to reveal it. Inside a carousel or an expanding block, the link usually has to be scrolled or expanded into view. An AI Overview link and an AI Mode link use those standard rules. Grouped by page, several links to the same page in one element are one impression. | Scrolling away and back, or paging away and back, in the same query or session. A query-refinement link. Search Labs experiments. A result on a page the user did not open: that result has no impression, and then no position. A visit’s booking or reply. |
| CTR | Clicks divided by impressions, for the grouping the report is using. Card 05: grouped by page, the Pages row is that page; the chart is the property. | A separate event. Clicks that the click row excluded. A conversion rate. A pass. Google publishes no CTR that means the page passed. Card 05. |
| Average position | The average of the topmost position of a link to the property or page, across the impressions that were recorded. Position 1 is the topmost position on the page. Each element occupies one position. An AI Overview occupies a single position, and every link in it is assigned that position. Ads do not occupy a search position. A link must get an impression for a position to be recorded. | Discover and News. A view with no impression: the report can show a dash, which is not position zero. Query-refinement elements that contain no outbound link: they do not occupy a position. Search Labs experiments. The position on one person’s search. The help page’s own example, that a report can say position 5 while one search shows position 8, is an explanation of averages. It is not a pass at position 5. |

**POLICY.** A click count is not a pass. An impression count is not a pass. CTR is not a pass. Average position is not a pass. The 12% CTR and the position-5 gap in `planner.py` on `main` are arithmetic in this repo. They are not numbers Google publishes. Card 07. This card does not put them in the YAML.

**MARKETING.** Any vendor page that states an SEO conversion rate. This card does not cite one, and it does not copy one into `actions` or into a queue item.

## 8. Business value

**POLICY.** The chain, in one line: impression, click, visit, action.

Search Console reports the impression and the click, plus CTR and average position as arithmetic on those two. The click is the person leaving Google for the page. The visit is that arrival. This card stores no separate visit counter and no percent between the steps. The action is a reply on the portfolio, or a booking or a call on the barber shop, from someone that site can actually serve. The operator writes the count on `actions`. The action is the pass. The four Search Console numbers are a report of the week. They do not increment `actions.count`.

An empty report, inside the 2–3 days or inside the week for a new property, is still a report. It is not a failed action, and it is not a passed action.

**Out.** Code. Connecting Search Console. A Business Profile edit. A real barber’s address. A mail URL the operator has not written. Card 10’s twelve implementation stages, restated as a build order.

## 13. Won't-chase

- A conversion percent, a 12% CTR, or position 5 as a pass.
- A Google-published page count. The 12 in §4 is this design.
- A day from publish until the first impression. That day stays **UNKNOWN**.
- Treating the starter guide’s “few weeks,” or the Search Console week, as this pass.
- A keyword field, or a query field.
- Local service-area pages on the portfolio.
- A suburb grid on the shop.
- Scraped ranks, invented reviews, invented prices, invented replies.

## 14. What would prove this wrong

1. The OECD withdraws “up to 249” and “fewer than 250,” or the EU recommendation withdraws the fewer-than-250 staff ceiling or the EUR 50 million, EUR 43 million, EUR 10 million, or EUR 2 million ceilings, where §3 quotes them.
2. A solo owner-manager is staff of 10 or more on the EU list, where §3 reads one full-time owner-manager as micro.
3. Google publishes a page count for scaled content abuse, where card 02 says that count is **UNKNOWN**. This card would then say whether 12 still stands as a design ceiling.
4. Google publishes a click count, an impression count, a CTR, or a position and says that number is a pass, where §7 says no such pass is published.
5. Google states a day from the publish of one URL until the first impression, where card 05 says that day is **UNKNOWN**.
6. Card 06 treats a new URL as the right response to a query, a click count, or an impression count moving.
7. Card 10 allows local service-area intent as a portfolio aim, where this card forbids it.
